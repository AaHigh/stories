VERIFIED FACTS

Courthouse Park Knife Attack:
Senior DDA Stabbed in Targeted Assault

Dustin Crawford, 42, of Fresno faces attempted murder charges after walking up to a senior deputy district attorney and stabbing him three times in the back during a daytime campaign film shoot.

Independent Report
High Tower District Community Archive

On the afternoon of Thursday, August 20, 2026, a senior Fresno County deputy district attorney was stabbed multiple times in the back while standing with a group filming a campaign commercial in Courthouse Park, directly outside the Fresno County Superior Courthouse. Authorities state the attack was not random. Surveillance video shows the suspect, Dustin Crawford, 42, of Fresno, walking straight to the victim and striking him. Crawford faces charges of attempted murder and attempted murder of a public official. Bail is set at $1 million. The victim was treated and released the following day.

TIMELINE

Sequence of Events

At approximately 1:13 p.m. on Thursday, August 20, 2026, Fresno County Sheriff’s Office dispatchers received reports of a man being stabbed in Courthouse Park, the open space between the Hall of Records and the main Superior Courthouse in downtown Fresno. The attack occurred near the Law Enforcement Memorial while a group was filming a television campaign commercial for Fresno County Superior Court judicial candidate Ashley Paulson.

The senior deputy district attorney was on break from his regular duties and standing with the campaign group. According to District Attorney Lisa Smittcamp and Sheriff John Zanoni, surveillance video captured the suspect walking directly toward the victim, bypassing other people nearby, and stabbing him several times in the back.

Fresno County Courthouse, 1100 Van Ness Avenue. Courthouse Park lies immediately in front of the building where the attack occurred.

Deputies assigned to the courthouse responded within minutes. The victim was found conscious, breathing, and talking. He was transported to Community Regional Medical Center. At 1:16 p.m. — three minutes after the initial call — deputies located the suspect near the intersection of Fresno Street and Van Ness Avenue. A Taser was deployed to take him into custody. A weapon was recovered.

The victim was released from the hospital the following day and is expected to make a full recovery. His name has not been released out of respect for him and his family.

MOTIVE

Targeted Retaliation

Sheriff Zanoni and District Attorney Smittcamp both stated that the attack was targeted, not a random act of violence. Video evidence shows Crawford singled out the victim. Smittcamp noted it is possible Crawford recognized the prosecutor as the person who handled his 2016 case.

In 2016, Crawford was prosecuted for assault with a deadly weapon, resisting arrest, and battery on a police officer stemming from a 2015 incident that included the stabbing of a neighbor. He accepted a plea and was sentenced to five years in state prison. He has a prior serious or violent felony conviction on his record.

Smittcamp emphasized: “This is not a random act of violence by a mentally ill man. This was targeted.” She has publicly criticized aspects of California’s criminal justice reforms in connection with the case, describing a pattern of insufficient accountability.

The Tulare County District Attorney’s Office is handling the prosecution to avoid any conflict of interest within the Fresno County DA’s Office.

CHARGES

Facing Life Exposure

Dustin Crawford is booked into Fresno County Jail on $1 million bail. Court filings list the following charges and enhancements:

Attempted willful, deliberate & premeditated murder
Attempted murder of a public official
Assault with a deadly weapon (knife)
Assault on a public official
Great bodily injury enhancements
Prior serious/violent felony allegation

If convicted on all counts as charged, Crawford faces the possibility of life in prison.

DISTRICT CONTEXT

Public Space, Daylight, Retaliation

Courthouse Park sits at the civic center of Fresno. It is a place where attorneys, staff, campaign workers, and the public routinely gather in plain view of the courthouse itself. A targeted daytime stabbing of a prosecutor while he was on break filming a judicial campaign commercial is an attack on the visible functioning of the justice system.

This incident follows earlier violence in the same park. In June 2026, another man was stabbed multiple times between the Hall of Records and the main courthouse; that suspect was booked on attempted homicide. The pattern of open-space violence immediately adjacent to the county’s primary court facilities raises ongoing questions about security posture and the cumulative effect of prior cases that return individuals with violent histories to the same streets and parks.

For residents of the High Tower District and the wider city, the facts remain straightforward: a prosecutor was selected, approached, and stabbed in the back in a public park in broad daylight. The suspect was in custody three minutes later. The case now moves through the courts under outside prosecution.
