Enjoy Yourself

Ten thousand views of a fifty-eight second short. On the street he stayed civil. The man who left the Honda wanted the attention. He got it.

The short
10,000 views
YouTube Short, channel AARON. Uploaded August 26. Milestone counted August 29.
The line
"Well, enjoy yourself."
Spoken on the street after he got out of the car. Spoken with the knowledge that a camera would outlive the argument.
The prior file
Echo and Thomas
Plate 9DZH843. The stop at Thomas and Broadway. The molestation inversion. The civic record is already published.

He seemed to want it

The first file was the incident. This file is the audience.

Three days after a driver ran the stop at Thomas and Broadway, left the car, walked toward the camera, told a cyclist to shut the fuck up, offered a fight, and then invented a molestation story for the neighborhood, the short that holds that minute crossed ten thousand views.

That is not a vanity number. It is proof that the behavior travels. People recognize it because they have stood on the same kind of curb. A man who cannot manage a dog in the road, a stop sign, or a two-hand slow-down signal is not a rare specimen in 93728. He is the ordinary cost of living here.

When the cyclist said "enjoy yourself," it was not a taunt tossed into a vacuum. It was a civil sentence spoken by somebody who already knew the rest of the minute would be performed for an audience. He did not swear at the driver. He did not take the Name. He named the speed, named PC 415, said he did not want a fight, and handed the man back his own behavior. The driver seemed to want the attention. He seemed to get it. He seemed to enjoy what he was doing the whole time he was doing it.

"Well, enjoy yourself."

The camera did the rest. Ten thousand people have now watched a grown man leave a Honda because someone told him, without a curse, to slow down.

The walk

This is the behavior. Not a crop. Not a blur. The open door, the bicycle on the ground, the walk toward the person who asked him to tap the brake.

The short

Play the minute. It is short on purpose. The whole argument fits inside a YouTube Short because the whole argument was a man who could not stay in his car.

The commandment is not in the minute

Play the short again if you need the proof. The recorder stayed civil. Slow down. I'm talking to you. You're speeding. That's a PC 415. I don't want to fight. You're in control of your own behavior, not me. Enjoy yourself. That is the file.

The man who recorded it is a religious man. The Third Commandment is not a style rule he keeps for church. "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain." Exodus 20:7. Deuteronomy 5:11. It is one of the ten. Violating it is a serious matter to him. Hurting somebody would be worse. Going to jail would be worse. He will not do those things. He controlled the minute on Echo Avenue because those costs are real.

The problem sits after the recording. The compulsion is to defend himself, and to defend a right not to be violated by predators in this neighborhood. That is when the Name comes out. Goddamnit. That is when he has to ask for forgiveness, not for a punch and not for a booking, but for verbal expressions uttered while trying to keep the same peace he kept on camera.

He should not have to ask forgiveness for words that only exist because the neighborhood keeps producing men who treat a civil warning as an insult.

That is the inversion he is trying to refuse. The driver lost his language and then reached for a sex-crime story. The recorder kept his language and later has to repent for the heat that arrives when he explains why the file exists at all. A man should be able to defend himself against predators without taking the Lord's name in vain. The fact that he can stay civil in the confrontation, and still finds himself asking forgiveness for the testimony afterward, is the measure of how much this place charges for an ordinary right.

Documenting the minute does not feel good. Ten thousand views of another man's inability to govern himself is not a party. The file exists. The walk is visible. The plate is checkable. The inversion he tried to plant, that the cyclist was "following people" in order to molest them, did not get to be the only copy in circulation.

This is not unusual

That is the whole point, and it is the part outsiders miss when they watch a short and think they are looking at a freak incident.

It is not unusual here. Reckless speed through stops that have already killed is not unusual. Men who treat a slow-down signal as a personal insult are not unusual. The move from "you're bothering me" to "shut the fuck up" to a sex-crime story is not unusual. What is unusual is putting a neck out far enough to record it while remaining civil, knowing the man coming out of the car might decide the argument is no longer verbal.

That is the real price. You tell somebody to slow down and you risk getting shot for the advice. You do it anyway because the alternative is pretending the pavement is safe when it is not. The Tower District has already paid in bodies for that pretense. Thomas and Broadway is not a theoretical intersection. A neighbor down the street had his back broken at Van Ness and Belmont by a driver running a signal. The people who live here are not inventing a danger to justify a video.

So the short stays up. The stills stay unblurred. The sentence "enjoy yourself" stays in the record as what it was: a civil line, spoken by a man thinking ahead, while another man performed his anger, that the performance would find an audience.

It found one. Ten thousand is only the first round number. The behavior is older than the view count, and it will outlast this page unless the neighborhood keeps making examples of it.

What this page is for

This is not a request that anyone hunt the driver. It is not a home address. It is not a name that was never spoken onto the recording. It is the public face of a public incident, published because the man who created the incident also tried to write the caption.

His caption was: watch out for the guy on the bike. The actual file is: watch the man who ran the stop, then got out, then reached for the worst word he could find.

The first story holds the transcript, the statute, and the 1992 Lanning material on what happens when people weaponize the language of child protection. This story holds the milestone, the civil minute, and the later cost of having to defend the right to make the file. They belong next to each other.

Close

He wanted the attention. He got ten thousand pairs of eyes. That is not cruelty. That is what happens when you leave the car to punish a person for asking you, civilly, to slow down, and somebody keeps the file.
