The 4.6-Cent Illusion

Austin Energy’s first-tier energy charge is 4.640 cents per kilowatt-hour. That line is real. It is not the bill. Here is every 200 kWh step from 200 to 2,000, on a $40,000 income, with the low-usage and low-income discounts both cities actually offer.

Austin energy line

4.640¢

First 300 kWh only. Energy charge. Not the bill.

Austin all-in at 850 kWh

13.5¢

About $115 a month after every rider and the $16.50 customer charge.

Fresno all-in at 850 kWh

~40¢

About $338 a month on PG&E E-1 with no CARE.

The 4.2-cent memory is close. It is not the bill.

Austin Energy does use cheap energy tiers. The current inside-city energy charge is 4.640 cents for the first 300 kWh, then 5.138 cents from 301 to 900, then 7.525 cents from 901 to 2,000. Older filings sat near 4.1 cents on that first step. So the 4.2-cent figure people remember is in the neighborhood of the energy line, not a fabrication.

Every Austin kWh also carries a Power Supply Adjustment, a regulatory charge, and community-benefit riders. Those add 6.525 cents on top of the energy line. Then there is a $16.50 monthly customer charge and about 1 percent city tax. That is why 200 kWh in Austin costs about $39, not $9.28. The energy line at 200 kWh really is 4.640 cents. The all-in rate at 200 kWh is 19.6 cents because the fixed charge is spread over so few kilowatt-hours.

Fresno sits in PG&E Baseline Territory R. The current bundled E-1 energy prices are 32.561 cents up to baseline and 40.702 cents above it, plus a $24.15 Base Services Charge if the household is not on CARE or FERA. That is the no-assistance case this story was asked to run.

What the discounts actually are

Austin does give a break for using less. That is the tier table: 4.640, then 5.138, then 7.525, then 10.884 cents on the energy line. The low-usage gift is real. It is also small next to the riders. Crossing 900 kWh is the first sharp step. Crossing 2,000 kWh is the second.

Austin CAP is the income program. Eligible households at or below 200 percent of federal poverty, or on SNAP, Medicaid, SSI, CHIP, Lifeline, CEAP, and related lists, get the customer charge waived, the CAP rider waived, and a 10 percent cut on kilowatt-hour charges. On 860 kWh, Austin Energy’s own budget example moved a $117 bill to about $86. That is a bigger gift than the cheap first tier, and it is not the same thing as the tier.

PG&E CARE is the Fresno income program. A 1 or 2 person household at $40,000 is under the current CARE cap of $43,280. This story still prints the no-assistance column because that is what was asked. If that household enrolled, the 850 kWh Fresno year drops from about $4,060 to about $2,200. Still more than Austin retail. Not the same cliff.

PG&E also has a baseline allowance, which is a low-usage tier by another name. In Territory R the summer baseline is 17.7 kWh per day, about 539 kWh in a 30-day month. Winter baseline is 10.4 kWh per day, about 317 kWh. Use under baseline and you stay on 32.561 cents. Go over and the rest is 40.702 cents. That is why Fresno’s all-in rate flattens near 40 cents once a house is living above winter baseline most of the year.

The $40,000 sentence

At 200 kWh, electricity takes 1.2 percent of $40,000 in Austin and 2.7 percent in Fresno. At 850 kWh it takes 3.5 percent in Austin and 10.2 percent in Fresno. At 2,000 kWh it takes 8.3 percent in Austin and 24.2 percent in Fresno.

That last Fresno number is a quarter of a $40,000 gross year, before rent, water, food, or tax, with no CARE. The 4.6-cent Austin line is not a lie. It is an incomplete sentence.
