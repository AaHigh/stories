When the Facts Change

Keynes asked what you do, sir, when the facts change. Sony walked off a slate. A publisher found another door. Prestige did not save the budget. The same shape sits on the Capitol steps. The children already lived the first draft.

Published September 10, 2026 · Tower District, Fresno

The question

When the facts change, I change my mind. What do you do, sir?

The operator move

Cancel the project. Find another partner. Keep the work alive. Do not pretend the first deal was a triumph.

The child ledger

Homicide charts are not the walk home. A signed bill in September does not unspend the years the street was the policy.

The canceled slate

There is a clean story from the game business. PHYSINT was a 2024 PlayStation partnership. In mid-June 2026 Sony canceled it. Kojima spent three months finding a new publisher. Xbox, already on OD with him, took the project. Development continues.

Sony has not released the internal math. These games are costly. Capture, schedule, and auteur scope compete for a limited slate. When return or fit no longer holds, a publisher walks. Kojima then changed partners to keep the work alive. Xbox extended a bet it already had.

Prestige is not a veto on budget. The facts changed. The parties changed course. Whether Sony regrets it is a later question.

That is how adults close a book that stopped working. They do not hold a press conference that converts the cancellation into a masterpiece.

What the position actually is

Here is the position from the High Tower District, stated without perfume.

The governor is not a steward of child safety. He is a high-status operator who spent years protecting a policy stack that made streets, schools, and neighborhoods worse for children, then pivoted when the politics and the numbers forced him to. The late enforcement money, the CHP teams, the Prop 36 era rhetoric, and this week’s chatbot and social media bills do not erase that record. They confirm it.

Call that criminal if you mean a man who uses power to protect a theory while other people’s kids eat the result. Call it a joke of epic proportions if you mean the claim that the aggregate of public safety for children improved because Sacramento can now print a chart.

The walk to school is the ledger. Not the dome.

Two kinds of safety

The office in Sacramento now advertises historic lows. Homicide down since 2019. Double-digit drops across categories in 2025. Lowest homicide rate since statewide reporting began in 1966. Those numbers can be true on paper and still miss the thing a parent means.

A child’s safety is whether the walk, the park, the store, and the alley are usable. It is whether a released sex offender is back on the block. It is whether fentanyl and open use sit next to the places kids pass. It is whether retail theft and disorder close the shops families use. It is whether schools actually run. It is whether the system treats a predator as a first-order problem instead of a paperwork category.

Aggregate violent crime can fall while those conditions stay bad in the places families actually live. Fresno is not the statewide average. The Tower District is not a talking point. Concentrated disorder is still disorder. A ten-year-old does not live inside a Department of Justice table.

The stack he governed

Prop 47 reduced many theft and drug offenses to misdemeanors in 2014. He did not write it. He governed under it for years and only moved after voters and visible failure made the old line expensive. Research from the Public Policy Institute of California associates the measure with roughly a 10 percent increase in unsheltered homelessness and a 7 to 8 percent increase in serious drug use as measured by overdoses, hospitalizations, and emergency visits, plus fewer court-referred treatments.

Add realignment. Add Prop 57. Add parole practices that put people who were supposed to stay locked up back out. David Allen Funston is the kind of name that ends the sermon. A serial child molester serving life terms, released under elder parole, charged again almost immediately. A retired judge called the pattern reckless. That is not a cable chyron. That is a specific failure mode. The state optimized for release and diagnosis. The child was not the unit of account.

If the metric is whether children are safer from predators and from street-level collapse, a governor who signs or maintains that machinery is not delivering safety. He is managing a theory.

The years you cannot reprint

California districts kept children out of classrooms longer than most of the country. Learning loss, isolation, and the collapse of daily structure hit children, not the people writing the orders. You can later sign an online safety package. You cannot un-lose those years. Childhood is a one-time window. That alone is a large negative on the ledger.

Then there is the cooperation question. In El Cajon, federal flags on unaccompanied minors sat on a desk. Local officials asked whether they could do welfare checks. The Attorney General’s office warned that using that information could violate sanctuary law. If the first duty is the child in front of you, that tradeoff is indefensible. Ideology that blocks a welfare check is not child safety. It is a brand protecting itself.

Rules for thee

The charge of criminal does not require a jumpsuit tomorrow. It requires a pattern.

French Laundry during lockdown, while ordinary households ate the rules. Behested payments in the hundreds of millions, including large sums directed toward his wife’s nonprofit. A former chief of staff who pleaded guilty to fraud. Appointments that later detonated. Federal interest in the orbit around him, which he calls a vendetta.

You do not have to prove he personally pocketed a check. The charge is that he runs a machine that protects status and ideology first, then asks the public to treat the resulting street conditions as compassion. For anyone who treats child exploitation and street predation as bright-line evils, that looks like moral corruption even when the lawyers say it is legal.

The course correction

When retail theft, fentanyl deaths, visible homelessness, and voter revolt made the old story expensive, the governor put money into CHP suppression teams, organized retail theft grants, and fentanyl interdiction talking points. He found new language for encampments. On this same day, September 10, 2026, he signed a package of child online safety bills. Chatbot audits. Addictive features barred for users under 16.

Those are real actions. They are also late. They arrived after years of the opposite emphasis.

Sony canceled PHYSINT when the slate math no longer worked. Kojima found another publisher. Xbox doubled a bet it already had. Prestige did not save the original deal.

Same shape here. Changing partners when the facts change is what a politician does. It does not prove the first strategy was good for children. It proves the first strategy stopped being politically tenable.

What would actually count

Not a press release on homicides. Not a ranking on gun-law scorecards. Not a chatbot auditor bill signed the same evening this story went up.

It would look like fewer released child molesters. Faster and more certain consequences for people who traffic or assault kids. Streets and schools that children can use. Treatment that actually happens instead of catch and release. No ideological veto on checking whether a specific child is being exploited.

On that list, the record from 2019 through the mid-2020s is easy to call a failure. Recent enforcement and online bills are damage control and positioning. They do not convert the prior years into a success.

Closing

What do you do, sir?

Official stats can fall and the child-safety claim can still be a joke if the thing that actually happens to kids on the ground is disorder, delayed accountability, and a governor who discovered enforcement after the facts changed.

Keynes changed his mind. Sony walked. Kojima kept the work alive under a new roof. That is adult conduct. What is not adult conduct is selling the cancellation as proof that the original slate was always for the children.

The High Tower District does not live in the dome. It lives on the sidewalk. The next ballot is still marked. Use both.
