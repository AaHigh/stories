Yipes! Stripes!

The Fabulous Rainbow Zebra Guide to Zebra-Striping Your Night (and Your Liver) in the High Tower District

Extra long • Extra funny • Extra gay • Extra intentional

Once upon a sticky Fresno night in the High Tower District, a zebra named Yipes (yes, that Yipes, the one from Fruit Stripe gum who used to give you two glorious seconds of flavor and a temporary tattoo of himself skateboarding) decided the local bars needed a new lifestyle practice.

Not just any practice.
Zebra-striping.

He showed up wearing a rainbow scarf, a matching bowtie, rainbow sneakers, and a mane that looked like a Pride parade got into a fight with a highlighter set. He was fabulous. He was comical. He was slightly judgmental about last call.

“Listen, queens, kings, and everyone in between,” Yipes announced while striking a pose that somehow managed to be both majestic and a little slutty. “Zebra-striping is the future of responsible debauchery. You paint alternating stripes. You alternate your financial transactions. One transaction = alcohol. Next transaction = abstinence. Repeat until the night is fabulous and you still remember how you got home.”

He called it the Transaction Abstinence Protocol (TAP for short, because of course it is).

The Official Rules According to Yipes
(who is very dramatic about rules)

  1. 1 Every time money leaves your wallet or phone for a drink, that counts as a Drink Transaction.
  2. 2 The very next financial (or even non-financial “I’m about to order again”) moment must be an Abstinence Transaction. Water. Soda. Mocktail. Just standing there looking pretty. Anything that is not more alcohol.
  3. 3 The floor, the bar top, the stools, the bathroom doors — anything that can hold paint — gets zebra-striped (preferably with a cheeky rainbow stripe every few black-and-white ones because this is Tower and we are not basic).
  4. 4 If you mess up the pattern, Yipes appears in the mirror of the bathroom and says “Yipes! Stripes!” in a tone that is both loving and deeply disappointing.

Yipes’ One-Zebra Goodwill Tour

Goldstein’s Mortuary & Delicatessen

Yipes walked in, looked at the steampunk boat-hull bar and the 45+ taps, and whispered, “No bodies, no pastrami… just stripes.” He painted subtle zebra lines along the edge of the bar so that every time someone ordered a flight they would have to step across a white stripe and consider a water. The patio got a full rainbow-zebra treatment. He told the staff the new house special should be called “Embalming Fluid… but Make It Alternate.” They laughed. He winked. It was gay as hell and perfect.

Vibez

Yipes nearly fainted from joy. “This place already understands the assignment.” He zebra-striped the dance floor in rainbow-adjacent black-and-white so that every other step was an abstinence step. The drag queens immediately started using the stripes as choreography marks. One of them yelled “Yipes! Stripes!” mid-lip-sync and the whole room lost it. Transaction Abstinence became the unofficial house rule on brunch days: mimosa, then water, then mimosa, then water. The staff started calling it “the Yipes method.”

Tipzy’s

Claudia and Annie already run a place that feels like family. Yipes painted the bar edge and the karaoke stage with thick, bold stripes. He declared that every time someone buys a michelada or a round of shots, the next “transaction” has to be a water or a “just watching my friends make fools of themselves” pause. Karaoke nights got a new tradition: if you forget the alternating rule, you have to sing “Yipes! Stripes!” to the tune of whatever song is playing. It is already legendary.

The Howlin’ Wolf

Art-deco elegance met cartoon zebra. Yipes carefully striped the gorgeous mirrored frames and the edge of the stage. He told the bartenders that every crystal punch bowl ordered must be followed by a collective table water toast. “Craft cocktails deserve craft moderation,” he said while adjusting his rainbow scarf under the moody lighting. The place looked even more glamorous with the stripes. Several people immediately started flirting with the zebra. He accepted the compliments graciously.

Strummer’s

Punk energy + zebra stripes = chaos in the best way. Yipes painted the stage edge and the booth dividers. He announced that every time a band starts a new song, that is a free “reset” — but if you buy a drink during the set, the next thing you do with money or intent must be non-alcoholic. The security guys started enforcing it with pure love and zero judgment. Joe Strummer would have approved of the discipline mixed with the nonsense.

Livingstone’s (“Stones”)

The classic pub got the full speakeasy-zebra treatment. Dark wood + black-and-white stripes + a single rainbow stripe running the length of the bar like a secret Pride runway. Yipes told the regulars that every time they ordered another round, the next “transaction” was a pause or a water. “You’ve been doing this since forever,” he said. “You can handle alternating. You’re professionals.” The French onion soup somehow tasted better under the new lighting of the stripes.

Sequoia at 777 East Olive Avenue

The Log. The legendary long wood bar. Yipes stood at one end, looked down the entire length, and nearly cried from aesthetic emotion. He painted the cleanest, most perfect zebra stripes anyone has ever seen on a bar top, with a soft rainbow accent every seventh stripe. “This is the flagship,” he declared. “Every pint, then every water. Every flight, then every pause. The Sequoia will lead the District into a new era of fabulous, functional drinking.” The brewmaster nodded. The regulars raised their glasses… then their waters… then their glasses again. Perfect.

Yipes’ Closing Manifesto
(delivered from the middle of Olive Avenue with a glittery caution cone)

“Zebra-striping is not about being boring.

It is about being extra on purpose.

It is about lasting longer, looking hotter, remembering the gossip, and still being able to walk your fabulous self home.

It is about supporting the bars that support us — Goldstein’s, Vibez, Tipzy’s, The Howlin’ Wolf, Strummer’s, Livingstone’s, and the mighty Sequoia at 777 Olive.

Paint the stripes.
Alternate the transactions.
Drink. Abstain. Drink. Abstain.
Stay gay. Stay funny. Stay in the Tower.

Yipes! Stripes!

Then he vanished in a puff of temporary-tattoo glitter, leaving behind only the scent of long-gone Fruit Stripe gum and a District that suddenly looked a lot more striped… and a lot more intentional.

Support the bars. Paint the stripes. Alternate the transactions. See you on the patio.
